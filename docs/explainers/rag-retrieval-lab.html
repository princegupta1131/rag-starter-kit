Check the question

Guard

A few simple rules run first: not empty, not too long, and no attempt to trick the AI. If a rule fails, we stop right away.

The question

Checks

    Why?

    Every later step costs time or money (AI calls). A bad input stopped here costs nothing.

    The science, simply

    No AI at all, just text rules, like a security guard checking a list at the door. It runs in under a millisecond.

    Where in the kit

    retrieval_service/steps/step1_guard.py. Limit: 1,000 characters. The blocked phrases are listed at the top of the file.
    2

    Was it asked before?

    Cache

    If the same question was asked in the last 10 minutes, the saved answer comes back instantly, with no AI call.

    Try itEXAMPLE TIMES
    First time–The full pipeline runs: about 4 seconds, mostly the two AI calls.
    →
    Same question again–Answered from memory.

    Why?

    Ten people asking "how many leave days?" shouldn't cost ten AI calls.

    The science, simply

    The memory is labelled with the question plus a fingerprint of the documents. Upload or delete a file and the fingerprint changes, so old answers are never reused by mistake.

    Where in the kit

    step2_cache.py. Setting: CACHE_TTL_SECONDS=600 (10 minutes).
    Go deeper: what is not cached

    Follow-up questions ("until when?") are never cached: their meaning depends on the conversation. The cache lives in the server's memory, so with several servers you'd move it to Redis.

    Make the question complete

    Query rewrite

    In a chat, people ask short follow-ups. A quick AI call turns them into a complete question, so the search knows what to look for.

    See it with a follow-upEXAMPLE

    "until when can unused annual leave be carried over"

    Why?

    "until when?" alone matches nothing in the handbook. The complete question finds the rule about 31 March.

    The science, simply

    Like a colleague asking "you mean the leave carry-over rule?". Only the search uses the rewrite; the answer still sees Priya's real words.

    Where in the kit

    step3_rewrite.py, prompt in prompts/rewrite.md. Turn off with QUERY_REWRITE=false.

    Turn the question into numbers

    Embedding

    The question goes through the same model as the handbook pieces, so it gets coordinates on the same meaning map. Now we can measure how close it is to every piece.

    See it: where the question landsREAL SCORES

      Why?

      Numbers from different models can't be compared, like GPS coordinates from two different maps. Same model = same map.

      The science, simply

      The model is told "this is a question" or "this is a document". Questions and answers are phrased differently, and this lands a question near the text that answers it.

      Where in the kit

      B · Find the right pieces
      5

      Search two ways

      Hybrid search

      Two searches run at once: one by meaning (the numbers) and one by exact words (the keywords). Each makes its own ranked list.

      See itREAL · POSTGRES
      By meaning · like a librarian who understands
        +
        By exact words · like Ctrl+F

        Why?

        Meaning search handles different words and languages. Word search catches exact things meaning can blur: product codes, names, acronyms like "2FA".

        The science, simply

        Meaning: the closest arrows (cosine, part 1). Words: Postgres reduces words to their roots ("carried" → carri) and finds pieces that contain them.

        Where in the kit

        step5_retrieve.py and the SQL in repositories/chunks.py. Each search returns up to 20 pieces.
        Go deeper: about this demo

        To make the ranking visible, this page treats each handbook sentence as a small piece (12 pieces). In the kit the handbook is 2 pieces of about 200 words, so its real scores are a little lower (around 0.57–0.65), because each piece mixes several topics.

        6

        Combine the two lists

        Rank fusion (RRF)

        The two lists are merged into one. A piece that is high in both lists goes to the top. The best 6 are kept.

        See it: the combined top 3REAL RANKS

          Why?

          The two searches score on different scales (0.75 vs 0.3), so their scores can't simply be added. Their positions can.

          The science, simply

          Like two judges ranking dancers: each judge gives points by position, and you add them up. 1st place in both = the winner.

          Where in the kit

          step5_retrieve.py: RRF_K = 60, and TOP_K=6 in .env. The 2 closest by meaning are always kept.
          Go deeper: the formula, for every piece

          Each list gives a piece 1 / (60 + its position). The 60 keeps the gap between 1st and 2nd place small, so one list can't overpower the other.

          7

          Is the best match good enough?

          Evidence gate

          If even the best piece is not similar enough (below 0.50), we stop and say "couldn't find this". The AI is never asked, so it can't make something up.

          Try it: move the barREAL SCORES

          Why?

          Without this, the AI would get loosely related text and might stitch together a confident wrong answer.

          The science, simply

          A bouncer at the door: below the bar, nobody goes in. But similar is not the same as answered: a piece can be close in topic and still not contain the answer. That's what step 8 catches.

          Where in the kit

          step6_answer.py. Settings: MIN_SIMILARITY=0.50, CONTEXT_MARGIN=0.12.
          Go deeper: which pieces go to the AI

          Only pieces within 0.12 of the best one (or found by exact words) are sent, so the AI isn't distracted by loosely related text. For this question:

            8

            Write the answer from the sources only

            Grounded generation

            The AI receives strict rules, the numbered pieces, and the question. It writes a short answer and marks every fact with its source, like [1].

            See itEXAMPLE ANSWER
            What the AI gets
            • Use only these sources, no outside knowledge
            • Cite every fact, like [1]
            • Reply in the question's language
            • No answer in the sources → reply NOT_FOUND
              →
              What the AI writes

              Why?

              An open-book exam: the AI may only use the pages it was given, and must say which page. That's what makes the answer trustworthy.

              The science, simply

              Large language models follow instructions in the prompt. Strict rules + only relevant text + "say NOT_FOUND if it's not there" = the second safety net, after step 7.

              Where in the kit

              The rules: prompts/answer_system.md (edit them to change tone or language). The call: step6_answer.py.

                Why?

                AI can invent a citation like [7] that doesn't exist. Checking means every source shown is real, so users can verify the answer themselves.

                The science, simply

                No AI here: plain code compares the numbers in the answer with the sources that were sent, like a teacher checking a bibliography.
